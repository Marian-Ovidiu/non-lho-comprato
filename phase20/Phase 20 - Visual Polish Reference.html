The thesis

NLC's aesthetic is flat editorial: full-bleed sections divided by hairline rules, no card shadows, mono tabular numerals, serif-italic asides, a single gold accent. It is deliberate and it works. The problem isn't the look — it's consistency. Two dialects coexist: the editorial-divided surfaces (dashboard, entries, form) and a card-filled dialect (quick-add sheet, category management on raw shadcn inputs). Phase 20 pulls everything onto one rhythm, one radius, one state model — and nudges the card dialect back toward editorial.

Rule

Refine within the system. Don't add cards, gradients, or shadows beyond true overlays. Don't move data (Phase 14 hierarchy holds). Don't strip a single Phase 18 a11y attribute — label semantics, aria-pressed, aria-describedby, focus-visible rings all stay. Visual style of a label may change; its HTML role may not.

Elevation

The app is flat by design — depth comes from hairlines, not shadow. Only true overlays carry elevation: --shadow-sheet (bottom sheets) and --shadow-pop (dialogs/toasts). Everywhere else: --shadow-none.

Entry list — row readability

Three text tiers stack in each row (title / mono meta / badge + detail). They're slightly cramped and the badge+detail crowds on narrow widths. The fix: comfortable tap rhythm, a more legible badge, and the comparison detail allowed to drop to its own line instead of fighting the badge.

Tap height
row padding settles at --sp-row-y 14 top & bottom → ≥48px hit area, comfy thumb rhythm
Badge
pill padding 2/8 → 3/9, weight 500, tracking 0.12em — readable at 10px
Detail line
comparison detail wraps under the badge on narrow widths, never clipped
Meta
mono meta line-height +1 for legibility; icon locked to muted-foreground
